The Digital Mediator: Policy Frameworks for Integrating Artificial Intelligence into Alternative Dispute Resolution

Keywords

Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) Generative AI Fifth Party Agent Algorithmic Sandboxing Automation Bias The Singapore Convention Punjab ADR Act 2019

Abstract

The international landscape of Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) finds itself on the cusp of a great technological paradigm shift. A successful mediation is always anchored entirely on the human element, such as a mediator’s ability to utilize emotional intelligence, empathy, and neutrality. However, the emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) and its predictive power has firmly established itself in the mediation room. Algorithms are no longer a passive “fourth party” — instead, they have evolved into an active “fifth party agent” capable of guiding negotiations and suggesting settlement options independently. This paper examines the profound policy and regulatory gap created by AI advancement by focusing on the procedural disclosure gap and the algorithmic-ingestion paradox, while analyzing how Large Language Models (LLM) clash with key mediation principles under the EU AI Act; the UNCITRAL Model Law and domestic frameworks like the Punjab ADR Act 2019 are also put to the test. What happens when a settlement under the Singapore Convention is mediated by an algorithm? This paper argues that the answer should worry us. Consequently, this paper argues that the integrity of the process cannot be compromised just for the sake of expeditious computational output. It proposes a solution via a step-by-step framework positioned on algorithmic sandboxing, transparent disclosure rules, and a mandatory human-in-the-loop standard to uphold the integrity of digital justice.

Associate Partner

Zill E Rukh Mushtaq is an Associate Partner at FARZIL LAW and Director of the FARZIL LAW Centre for Dispute Resolution (FLCDR). He holds an LLB from the University of the Punjab, Lahore, and a Bachelor’s degree in Pakistan Studies from Allama Iqbal Open University, Islamabad. He is an Accredited Mediator (IMI, CMC, SIMI) and an Associate Arbitrator certified by ADR ODR International Ltd. (UK), and serves as Chapter Head (Lahore) of AIPC (ADR ODR International × ICDRL Peacemakers Community). His work focuses on ADR, mediation and arbitration, citizenship & immigration law, and corporate advisory. He has completed training programmes at LUMS and Harvard Law School and is affiliated with CIArb, ABA, and ASIL.
